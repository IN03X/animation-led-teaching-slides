# Cold-start rotation lesson

Authority: the current user's prompt only. No course folder, project documents,
conversation history, external sources, or other skills are consulted.
Skill reference assets are private template evidence, never topic evidence.

Known: Cartesian coordinates; basic signed sine and cosine. No matrices.
P=(x,y), centre C=(a,b), displacement Q=(u,v)=(x-a,y-b).
Q'=(U,V) after a counterclockwise angle theta; P'=C+Q'.
Positive x right, positive y up; both axes have identical scale.
Concrete: P=(4,2), C=(2,1), Q=(2,1), theta=90 degrees,
Q'=(-1,2), P'=(1,3).
General: U=u cos(theta)-v sin(theta), V=u sin(theta)+v cos(theta).
Justification: split a displacement into two head-to-tail signed legs.
Rotation preserves a translated arrow's displacement and head-to-tail joins.
The horizontal unit direction becomes (cos(theta),sin(theta)); the perpendicular
vertical unit direction is its quarter-turn (-sin(theta),cos(theta)). Scale each
by its signed coordinate and add. Negative legs reverse their arrows. At Q=0
both legs vanish, so the centre stays fixed. No polar angle is required.
Use the returned rule for the SAME P,C at theta=30 degrees:
P'=(3/2+sqrt(3), 2+sqrt(3)/2), approximately (3.2321,2.8660).
